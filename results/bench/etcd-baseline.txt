Measurement scope:
  Both systems ran on this Linux host. The container runtime and
  filesystem facts above are part of the comparison provenance.

  The mechanism behind any difference is not a mystery: etcd stores in
  bbolt, a B+tree with a per-transaction fsync, and speaks gRPC over
  HTTP/2. Keel stores in an LSM with group commit, where one fsync
  retires every batch queued behind it, and speaks length-prefixed
  frames over TCP. Different trades, not different amounts of effort.

--- durability and runtime caveat

Keel uses fdatasync for durable batches; etcd runs through the recorded
container runtime. This is a same-host comparison, not proof that their
storage engines have identical durability paths.

The honest internal control is Keel's own fsync-off arm:
results/bench/ablation-fsync-off.txt, where the same cluster with writes
neither ordered nor durable meets the same offered rates within two
percent, at about half the tail latency, and keeps holding its schedule
past the rate at which the durable arm bends. That is the cost of the
promise, measured on one machine with one variable changed — and it is
latency and headroom rather than throughput, because one flush now
retires a batch of tens rather than a single entry.
