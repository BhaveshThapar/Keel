--- what this buys a CI job

A GitHub-hosted ubuntu-latest runner is slower than this host, by an
amount nobody here has measured: the repository has no remote yet, so
no workflow has ever run and there is no observed job duration to
divide by. The budgets below divide the slowest measured rate by a
factor of 6 and spend at most 25 of each job's 45 minutes on
sweeping. That factor is an assumption, and is labelled as one.

It is an assumption with a mechanism to replace it: the throughput job
in .github/workflows/nightly.yml runs this same script on the runner
and uploads the result. The first time it runs, the factor becomes a
measurement and this paragraph goes away.

simulator job    slowest cell default at 3 nodes          708592 steps/s
                 at 50000 steps:  3500 seeds is the 25-minute ceiling
                                   700 seeds per shard is what CI asks for
disk-simulator   slowest cell disk-chaos at 5 nodes       288288 steps/s
                 at 40000 steps:  1800 seeds is the 25-minute ceiling
                                   350 seeds is what CI asks for

CI asks for the 5-minute figure, not the ceiling. The five-fold gap is
margin against the runner factor being wrong, and it is deliberate: a
budget spent to its limit fails the day the runners get slower, and a
red build nobody can attribute to a code change is worse than a sweep
that is narrower than it could be.

The measurement's finding is that the counts these replace — 250 per
shard and 120 on the disk job — were low by roughly a factor of three,
not high. Sizing by eye was conservative rather than reckless, but it
was still unsupported, and P19's distinct-seed target spends against
this same file rather than against another guess.
