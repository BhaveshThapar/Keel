The spread is the measurement, not noise around one. Failover here is
bimodal — which node campaigns first decides which mode a trial lands
in — so p10 and p90 say more about what a client sees than p50 does,
and p50 is the one statistic that sits between the modes.

the median is stable: 932.2 ms over the first half of the trials and
931.6 ms over the second. That check is here because the count alone was
not enough — this distribution is bimodal and a hundred trials put the
median on either side of the fence.

The clock starts when the kill signal is sent and stops when a client's
write is acknowledged — not when a new leader is elected. Election is an
internal event a client cannot observe, and it is strictly earlier: the
new leader must also commit its own term's no-op before it can serve.
